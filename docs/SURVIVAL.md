# Survival analyses

Choose **Time until an event** on the design board, or open the simulated **Baseline treatment and event-free follow-up** example. Assign three distinct columns: baseline treatment `0/1`, strictly positive elapsed duration from a common time zero, and single event `1` / right censoring `0`. An optional person identifier must be unique. AFT additionally accepts baseline confounders; columns marked forbidden are excluded explicitly. Define the event and duration unit before estimation.

Select a probability difference at a horizon, a restricted mean survival time (RMST) difference through a horizon, or a conditional AFT time factor. Every method requires an explicit duration unit (`days`, `weeks`, `months` or `years`). Probability/RMST analyses also require a positive common horizon in those units, no longer than observed follow-up in either group. A sparse risk set is flagged. The horizon should be chosen for scientific relevance before inspecting the contrast; common maximum follow-up does not establish conditional treatment or censoring support.

| Adapter | Reported quantity | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `survival.kaplan_meier` | Observed group Kaplan–Meier probability difference at tau | Unadjusted association; independent censoring within each group required |
| `survival.rmst` | Area under each observed Kaplan–Meier curve through tau, treatment 1 minus 0 | Unadjusted event-free time contrast in duration units |
| `survival.aft` | Average model-predicted survival/RMST setting baseline treatment to 1 and 0 for every complete-case covariate profile | Counterfactual standardization only under justified causal, censoring and model assumptions |
| `survival.aft` with `time_ratio` | `exp(beta_treatment)` in a common-scale log-time model | Conditional common event-time factor; model association unless a causal interpretation is justified separately |

The AFT adapter fits a right-censored log-normal or Weibull likelihood. Event records contribute a density; censored records contribute survival probability. By default the model is `log(T) = beta0 + betaA A + betaL L + sigma epsilon`, with common scale. An optional prespecified baseline subgroup adds all modifier main effects and treatment-by-modifier interactions, as described below. Numeric baseline adjustment columns and categorical adjustment indicators are centered/scaled; their coding and fitted coefficients appear in result tables. Subgroup indicators use explicit reference coding. Weibull shape is `1/sigma` and its covariate-specific scale is `exp(mu)`, matching the location-scale convention documented by [R survival](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/survival/html/survreg.html).

For marginal targets, both treatment predictions use the same complete-case empirical baseline population. The curve, target population, duration unit and horizon accompany the estimate. Consistency, no interference, exchangeability, treatment positivity, independent censoring conditional on treatment/covariates and correct AFT model form remain untested. Complete-case exclusions are counted and can change the population; converged likelihood or similar estimates cannot establish identification. The conditional time factor is different from a marginal probability effect, hazard ratio, structural failure-time estimate or RMST ratio at a fixed finite horizon. Under a common time factor `r`, `RMST1(tau) = r RMST0(tau/r)`, not generally `r RMST0(tau)`.

Intervals use reproducible subject resampling. Kaplan–Meier resamples independently within observed arms and recomputes the curve/integral. A draw ending before the horizon is usable when its KM curve reaches zero; a positive surviving tail without follow-up is unsupported. AFT resamples people, refits the censored likelihood and re-averages over the resampled empirical target population in every draw. The default is 100 draws; 20–2000 are allowed and at least 80% must be usable. Failure counts are disclosed. More draws reduce simulation noise but cannot fix sparse support or incorrect identification. AFT requires full-rank covariates, event support, a finite interior optimum and stable positive likelihood curvature; unusable fits produce an explanatory failure.

These adapters support independent people, baseline binary treatment, one event and right censoring. Delayed entry, interval censoring, competing-event codes, recurrent events, person-period data, time-varying treatment, clustered observations and weights are refused rather than silently ignored. Other estimators can address those settings, but are outside this implementation. Do not recode competing death as ordinary censoring to bypass the guard. Align eligibility, baseline assignment and follow-up to avoid immortal-time bias.

The example is generated from original local simulation code and redistributes no study rows. Its fixed eight-month RMST target is computed from the known data-generating distribution. Baseline risk confounds treatment, so raw RMST intentionally misses the standardized target. Identification and model conditions hold by construction in that simulation; real studies need independent substantive justification.

## Prespecified baseline subgroup heterogeneity

All three adapters support an optional `subgroups: ["baseline_group"]` option and require `subgroups_prespecified: true` when it is selected. The app exposes these controls on each method. Select one categorical baseline modifier with 2–8 observed levels, measured before treatment, and specify the modifier, contrast scale, horizon and analysis before inspecting outcomes. This declaration is recorded as analyst supplied and unverified; it cannot establish that a variable is baseline or that the analysis was prespecified. Automatic cutpoints, continuous modifier searches and multiple simultaneous modifiers are outside this implementation. Repeated runs require a separate prespecified multiplicity plan.

Modifier missingness enters the complete-case ledger. Each treatment-by-subgroup cell needs at least five complete subjects; AFT also requires three observed events per cell, a full-rank interaction model and adequate overall event support. A survival/RMST horizon must fall within observed follow-up in every cell. These checks cannot establish conditional positivity, independent censoring or identification. Raw subgroup KM requires independent censoring within **each treatment-by-subgroup cell**; independence within pooled treatment arms and within subgroup cells are distinct requirements. Sparse subgroup risk sets are flagged.

| Adapter and selected target | Subgroup contrast | Direct heterogeneity null |
|---|---|---|
| KM survival difference | `S(tau | A=1,Z=z) - S(tau | A=0,Z=z)` from observed subgroup curves | Equal probability differences across levels at this horizon |
| KM RMST difference | Difference of observed subgroup curve areas through `tau` | Equal RMST differences across levels, in the stated duration units |
| AFT standardized survival/RMST | Both intervention predictions averaged over the same complete-case profiles within each subgroup | Equal standardized differences on the selected marginal scale and horizon |
| AFT conditional time factor | `exp(betaA + betaAZ[z])` in a pooled interaction model | Equal log time factors across levels; ratios remain displayed with null one |

For AFT the fitted model contains the intercept, treatment, baseline adjustment, `K-1` categorical modifier main effects and all `K-1` treatment interactions. A modifier already named as a confounder is represented once by those categorical main effects. Residual scale remains common. In a time-factor run the headline explicitly describes the reference subgroup; the subgroup table reports every factor. Marginal runs retain the overall standardized headline and display each subgroup's standardized survival and RMST, with pointwise intervals, alongside conditional time factors. Only the selected estimand receives the omnibus test. Variation in marginal differences can occur even with equal log time factors, so scale-specific tests answer different questions.

KM/RMST resample subjects independently within treatment-by-subgroup cells. AFT resamples subjects within baseline subgroups, conditioning on observed subgroup sizes, refits the **entire pooled interaction model**, and re-averages resampled profiles within each subgroup. All subgroup estimates remain together in each bootstrap vector. With `C` the matrix of `K-1` subgroup-minus-reference contrasts and `V` the covariance of the joint bootstrap estimates, the approximate omnibus statistic is `(C theta)' (C V C')^-1 (C theta)`, compared with chi-square `K-1`. For time factors the calculation uses their logarithms. This is a direct equality test; comparing separate subgroup significance decisions is invalid. Singular or unstable contrast covariance produces an explicit unavailable test with no p-value. At least 80% of requested draws must be usable; unusable fits, insufficient support and Monte Carlo precision concerns are disclosed. AFT replicates with inadequate treatment/event support are refused. Fewer than 200 usable draws triggers a precision warning; increasing draws addresses Monte Carlo error, not sampling bias or unsupported assumptions.

The app presents subgroup estimates, direct contrasts against the reference, pointwise percentile intervals, a forest plot and the omnibus diagnostic. Ratio runs display ratios of conditional time factors in the direct comparison table. These intervals are **not** adjusted for simultaneous subgroup comparisons or searches across methods, scales and horizons. A large omnibus p-value does not prove homogeneous effects, and a small one does not establish causal effect modification. Raw KM contrasts remain associations; AFT standardization requires the same explicit causal, censoring and model assumptions within each subgroup. Patient-specific effects, data-driven subgroup discovery, clustered sampling and informative censoring corrections are not implemented here.

The distinction between subgroup tests and a test of their difference follows [Altman and Bland (2003), Interaction revisited](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.326.7382.219). Baseline definitions, interaction testing, prespecification and multiplicity are discussed by [Wang et al. (2007), Reporting of Subgroup Analyses in Clinical Trials](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsr077003). These references motivate the interpretation and safeguards; the bootstrap Wald implementation is assessed by the independent numerical tests, not certified by either publication.

Methodological context: [Kaplan and Meier (1958)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1958.10501452), [Wei (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.4780111409), [Hernán (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181c1ea43), [Uno et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2014.55.2208), and [Causal Inference: What If](https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook). These sources motivate definitions and distinctions; their citation is not validation or endorsement of this adapter. Metadata and original project explanations are distributed, not paper bodies.

Numerical coverage in `tests/python/test_survival.py` uses tied-event product-limit hand arithmetic, independent survival-distribution quadrature, uncensored log-time OLS, independently optimized censored density/survival likelihoods, full bootstrap refitting, target averaging and adversarial observation processes. App tests verify the time-ratio null at one, distinct scales and causal framing. Software checks do not validate a particular study's causal assumptions.

`tests/python/test_survival_heterogeneity.py` additionally checks subgroup KM against empirical survival and truncated-mean arithmetic, reproduces seeded joint-bootstrap covariance and omnibus statistics, checks AFT interactions against independent log-time OLS and survival quadrature, exercises censored Weibull fits, and refuses unsupported cells, horizons, declarations and covariance ranks.
