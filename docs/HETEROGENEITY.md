# Heterogeneity across outcome types

Choose a randomized or observational baseline design and the CATE or GATE question. Open **Treatment heterogeneity across outcome types** for a synthetic example, or assign your own outcome and treatment columns. Select the matching method and one baseline modifier. Declare the modifier, coding, outcome and contrast before inspecting effects. The declaration records your decision; it does not certify a preregistration or variable timing.

| Outcome | Method | Conditional interaction test | Outcome-scale test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous | `heterogeneity.continuous` | OLS treatment-by-modifier coefficients; HC3 | Equality of standardized mean differences |
| Binary | `heterogeneity.binary` | Logistic coefficients on the log-odds scale; HC0 | Equality of risk differences, risk ratios or marginal odds ratios |
| Count / rate | `heterogeneity.count` | Poisson conditional-mean coefficients on the log-mean/rate scale; HC0 | Equality of standardized count/rate differences or ratios |
| Nominal / ordinal | `heterogeneity.categorical` | Multinomial log-odds coefficient block; HC0 | Joint equality of category-probability differences |
| Event-free probability | `survival.kaplan_meier` | Not applicable; observed subgroup curves | Equality of unadjusted probability differences at a common horizon |
| Restricted event-free time | `survival.rmst` | Not applicable; observed subgroup curves | Equality of unadjusted RMST differences through a common horizon |
| Censored AFT | `survival.aft` | Pooled model includes treatment-by-category interactions; no separate coefficient-block test | Equality of conditional time factors or subgroup-standardized survival/RMST contrasts on the selected scale |

The non-survival methods include treatment and modifier main effects, treatment-by-modifier interactions, and selected baseline adjustment covariates. Categorical modifiers need 2–8 declared levels. Continuous modifiers retain a linear interaction and require two explicitly selected values within the observed range of both arms. The marginal test compares those two values; it does not test every possible nonlinear effect-modification function. Common ranges and minimum counts cannot establish local overlap.

Each non-survival result separates a conditional link-scale coefficient test from the selected outcome-scale test. The selected outcome-scale equality null is primary; the coefficient test is a supporting descriptive model diagnostic, not another opportunity to make a confirmatory claim. These are different null hypotheses. In particular, a constant odds ratio can coexist with varying absolute risk differences. Categorical subgroups average over their own baseline profiles, so different covariate composition can contribute to marginal contrast differences. Continuous values use the same baseline profiles. Ordinal outcomes are handled as categories without numeric scoring or a proportional-odds restriction; this preserves the outcome distribution at the cost of additional parameters.

Counts must be nonnegative integers. With no exposure column the model targets counts over a comparable observation window. A positive, fixed baseline exposure column supplies a log offset; predictions target rates per one exposure unit. Rate differences have units of counts per exposure unit; rate ratios are dimensionless. Treatment-dependent follow-up or person-time is unsupported by this rate interpretation. Robust Poisson inference allows variance misspecification while still requiring a correct conditional mean and dependence specification. It does not fit a negative-binomial dispersion model or a zero-inflation mechanism.

HC3 applies to OLS; GLM and multinomial inference explicitly use HC0. Optional cluster sandwich inference uses a finite-sample scaling correction, requires at least twenty clusters and five represented clusters per treatment-by-modifier cell, and remains asymptotic. These thresholds are software support checks, not proof of adequate effective information. Repeated person records, survey weights, blocked randomization, panel treatments, IV/RD/DiD interactions and longitudinal treatment regimes require their own design-specific methods; these four baseline models reject incompatible roles.

Joint Wald tests use the full covariance of direct differences. The categorical outcome test omits one redundant category because probabilities sum to one. Rank-deficient covariance yields an unavailable p-value, without silently reducing the scientific null. A significant result in one subgroup and a non-significant result in another is not evidence that the effects differ. A non-significant interaction does not establish equal effects. For the four non-survival adapters, pairwise comparisons receive Holm correction across all displayed pairs and outcome categories; their confidence intervals remain pointwise. Their two omnibus tests are unadjusted. Survival methods expose one selected-scale omnibus test and unadjusted pointwise contrasts against the reference subgroup. Additional outcomes, scales, modifiers, horizons or repeated analyses require a broader multiplicity plan.

Non-survival intervals use the sandwich covariance and delta method conditional on the empirical baseline profiles. They do not include sampling variation in a new target covariate distribution. Ratios are tested on the log scale and have null value one. The overall headline for continuous, binary and count outcomes summarizes the complete-case population; subgroup tables and direct tests answer the heterogeneity question. Categorical outcomes report a vector, with no fabricated scalar headline. The app, comparisons and reports retain family, scale, modifier, exposure and interpretation.

Survival methods use the resampling, censoring and AFT model checks described in [Survival analyses](SURVIVAL.md). Raw KM/RMST subgroup contrasts remain associations. AFT subgroup standardization requires causal identification, conditional censoring and model assumptions; a conditional time factor is a separate quantity. No Cox, competing-risk, recurrent-event or time-varying-treatment heterogeneity estimator is implied.

Existing causal forests and meta-learners also provide calibration/BLP, grouped effects and ranking summaries. These answer different questions from prespecified regression interactions. Their local held-score approximations do not inherit every theorem of published honest or independently evaluated RATE procedures. See the method-specific diagnostics and the knowledge graph's audit questions before interpreting learned heterogeneity as validated targeting performance.

The independent knowledge package and read-only MCP expose these methods through coverage, method requirements, evidence, context and audit-checklist queries. Checks start as `not_assessed`; retrieving a checklist does not execute an analysis or establish causal validity. The portable skill connects the guidance to authorized source-code inspection. Original summaries and bibliographic metadata are distributed; external paper bodies are not included.
